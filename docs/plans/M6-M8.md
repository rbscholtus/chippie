# Chippie: implementation plans for M6 and M8

## Context

M1–M5 and M7 are merged and deployed. The app plays CHIP-8, SUPER-CHIP and
XO-CHIP ROMs from a catalogue of 268 downloadable ROMs, but the TV is still
the prototype's plain CRT picture with text buttons under it. M6 puts the CRT
inside the generated 1970s TV with working controls. M8 sends what we learned
back upstream.

**Order: M6 → M8, but M8 does not depend on M6** (it is mostly writing and
checking ROMs in the current app), so it can run alongside.

| Dependency | Kind | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Art → M6 | Hard | The cabinet and its measurements (below) are in hand |
| M7 → M6 | Seams | Volume acts on the one `sound.Output` voice; tint uses `Renderer.SetPalette` |
| M5, M7 → M8 | Soft | Platforms of missing ROMs are confirmed by running them in Chippie |

Decisions made while planning (2026-11-14):

- **Knob art is cut from the cabinet.** The cabinet already has knobs painted
  in, in a style (chrome caps, a bar knob in the VHF dial, a grey rocker) that
  differs from the separately generated knobs. Cutting them out keeps lighting,
  style and scale identical, and their resolution is exactly what's needed.
  The separate knob images in `assets/source/` stay unused.
- **Channel numbers are drawn in code.** Both the cabinet's VHF ring
  (1 2 3 4 5 7 8 6 10 18 11 12 13) and the separate dial (13, 5 and 9 twice)
  have garbled numbers. The art tool erases the printed numbers from the ring,
  and the app draws `U 2 3 … 13` itself in a period font.
- **Grey studio background.** The cabinet keeps its light grey studio and floor
  shadow. The page around it is filled with the matching grey, and the crop
  edges fade into that colour so there's no seam. The Gemini sparkle mark is
  painted out with the surrounding background.
- **Library and Settings stay as buttons under the TV**, restyled, plus the
  existing keyboard shortcuts. No hotspots on the cabinet.
- **UHF dial = speed** (fine tuning), **VHF dial = channel**, as README §11.

**First step:** save this plan as `docs/plans/M6-M8.md` and link it from README
§19.

**Definition of done:** as before: lint native and `GOOS=js GOARCH=wasm` with
no bare `//nolint`, `go test -race ./...`, native and WASM builds, Playwright,
README/CONTRIBUTING updated, Conventional Commits, benchstat for `app/crt/`
changes. Branches `m6-tv` and `m8-upstream`, off `main`.

## M6: The 1970s TV

**Goal:** README §19's M6 row: generated art in, `skin.json`, knobs
(click/drag/wheel), power animation, OSD, remote on touch devices, HiDPI
layout, CRT at 60 FPS on iPhone.

### The art and its measurements

The cabinet (`assets/source/Gemini_Generated_Image_ixja8tixja8tixja.png`) is
2400×1792. Measured in that image's pixels (±3 px; final values are tuned with
the calibration overlay below):

| Part | Shape | Position | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screen (black area) | Rounded rectangle, edges bulge slightly | x 330, y 331 | 1306 × 1046 |
| VHF dial = channel (ring + bar knob, turn together) | Circle | centre (1905, 492) | r 104 (knob r 50) |
| UHF dial = speed (scale ring fixed, knob turns) | Circle | centre (2107, 492) | ring r 52, knob r 26 |
| Volume knob (tick marks fixed on cabinet) | Circle | centre (1850, 727) | r 35 |
| Tint knob | Circle | centre (1981, 732) | r 31 |
| Power rocker | Rectangle | x 2073, y 686 | 70 × 95 |

**Sprite sizes follow from this:** each control's sprite is its size in
cabinet pixels at `@3x`, two thirds of that at `@2x`, one third at `@1x`:

| Sprite | @3x | @2x | @1x |
|---|---|---|---|
| Channel dial | 212 × 212 | 141 | 71 |
| Speed knob | 56 × 56 | 37 | 19 |
| Volume knob | 74 × 74 | 49 | 25 |
| Tint knob | 66 × 66 | 44 | 22 |
| Power rocker | 72 × 97 | 48 × 65 | 24 × 32 |

(A 2–4 px margin over the painted size so the sprite fully covers it.)

### Art pipeline: `cmd/skin-gen`

A Go tool, run by hand when the art changes. Its input is `assets/source/`
(gitignored, large) plus a small committed recipe,
`assets/tv/recipe.json`, holding the measurements above in source
coordinates. It writes the committed outputs to `assets/tv/`:

- **Cabinet:** crop to the TV plus a margin, paint out the sparkle mark, fade
  the crop edges into the flat studio grey (sampled; also written to
  `skin.json` as `background`), and save `cabinet@1x/@2x/@3x.jpg` (~800,
  ~1600 and full width; under ~1 MB at @3x).
- **Controls:** cut each one out with a circular (or rounded-rectangle) mask
  with a 1 px soft edge, centred on its spindle so rotation is about the right
  point. Saved as `knob-*@Nx.png`, ~50 KB or less each.
- **Channel ring:** erase the printed numbers in the ring's annulus (pixels
  brighter than the ring, filled from the ring colour at the same radius),
  keeping the bar knob.
- **UHF scale:** same erase for its tiny garbled labels; the ticks stay.
- **`skin.json`:** written in `@3x` (cropped) coordinates from the recipe, so
  the two never disagree.
- Scaling uses `golang.org/x/image/draw.CatmullRom` (already a dependency via
  the Go fonts).

### `skin.json` and `app/skin`

- New package `app/skin`: parse and validate `skin.json` (README §12.1 format
  plus `background`, `ring` for the channel numbers, and per-control `kind`:
  `dial`, `knob`, `rocker`). Validation: everything inside the cabinet, no two
  controls overlapping, steps > 0.
- **Hit testing:** each control's hit area is its shape, grown to at least
  44 pt across (Apple's minimum). Where grown areas overlap (volume and tint
  are 24 pt apart on a phone), the nearest centre wins.
- **Loading:** fetched with `platform.Fetch` after start, as README §12.2 says,
  so the art doesn't grow the WASM. Native builds read `assets/tv/` from disk,
  as `app/roms` does for hosted ROMs. Only the size closest to the TV's real
  pixel width is fetched. Until it arrives, today's layout (CRT only) shows;
  then the cabinet fades in. Fix the `assets/assets.go` comment, which says the
  art is embedded.

### Layout and HiDPI (`app/ui.go`, `app/game.go`)

- **Landscape:** the TV centred and scaled to fit, the rest filled with
  `background`. **Portrait:** the TV at the top, scaled to the width, the
  remote below.
- The CRT picture is drawn into the screen rectangle from `skin.json`, scaled
  with the TV; the shader's curvature is set from it so the picture follows the
  tube's bulge.
- All sizes are in real pixels (`Layout` already multiplies by
  `DeviceScaleFactor`); the `@Nx` choice uses TV width × scale.
- Status line → restyled "Library" and "Settings" buttons under the TV.

### Controls

| Control | Click / tap | Drag | Wheel | Keyboard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Channel (VHF) | One step: right half = next game, left half = previous | Turn around the centre, a step per 360°/13 | One step per notch | PageUp / PageDown |
| Speed (UHF) | One step | Vertical drag, continuous | Fine steps | `[` / `]` |
| Volume | One step | Vertical drag over −135°…+135° (the painted ticks) | Steps | `-` / `=` |
| Tint | Next preset | — | Next/previous | `T` |
| Power | Toggle | — | — | `P` |

- **Channel:** 13 click-stops (`U`, 2–13), turning forever. Each step moves to
  the next/previous game in the library's current filter; the dial's position
  is decorative. The OSD shows the game's real channel number (its place in
  the list).
- **Speed:** ticks per frame within the platform's range; the default is the
  catalogue's value.
- **Volume:** acts on `sound.Output`; 0 mutes. Saved in preferences.
- **Tint:** cycles ROM colours → amber → green → white → Timendus, through
  `Renderer.SetPalette`. Saved per ROM, like settings.
- **Power:** off pauses the machine and plays the existing collapse animation;
  on resumes. The rocker sprite is flipped vertically for the "on" state, and
  a small amber pilot lamp is drawn beside it when on.
- Knob highlights rotate with the knob. If that looks wrong at full size,
  fall back to rotating only the grip ring and keeping the cap's highlight
  fixed (two sprites per knob).
- Settings that the knobs now cover (speed) stay in the Settings panel too,
  in sync.

### OSD

- On channel change: channel number and title, green, top-left of the
  picture, for ~2 s, as on period sets and in the prototype. On volume, tint
  and speed change: a short bar or label.
- Drawn into the picture before the CRT pass, so it curves and glows with it.
- **Fonts (README §20):** OFL only. Proposed: VT323 for the OSD, Jost (a
  Futura-like face matching the cabinet's labels) for the dial numbers. Both
  recorded in `assets/LICENSES.md`.

### Remote on touch devices

- Shown below the TV in portrait (beside it in landscape) once a touch is seen,
  or from Settings. A 4×4 hex keypad in the CHIP-8 layout, drawn in code in a
  1970s remote style (no art needed).
- Multi-touch through `ebiten.AppendTouchIDs`, so two keys can be held.
- If the ROM has a `keys` hint, its keys are highlighted (README §13).
- The library drawer covers the remote when open.

### Calibration overlay

- `?skin=debug` (browser) or F3 (native): draws every control's shape, hit
  area and centre, and the screen rectangle, over the art. Natively,
  `skin.json` reloads when it changes, so values can be tuned while looking.

### Performance

- The cabinet is one image draw per frame; knobs are a few small draws. If
  profiling on iPhone shows a cost, cache cabinet + knobs in an offscreen
  image redrawn only when a control or the size changes.
- Measure 60 FPS on iPhone with hi-res and 2-plane XO-CHIP games
  (e.g. Akir8, Turnover '77) after merge, as M7's notes planned.

### Tests

- `app/skin`: parse/validate (good file, each failure), `@Nx` choice, hit
  testing incl. grown areas and nearest-centre on overlap, angle and step maths
  (click halves, drag, wheel, wrap-around).
- `cmd/skin-gen`: masks are circular and centred, number erase leaves the ring
  colour, `skin.json` coordinates match the recipe after crop and scale.
- Layout: portrait and landscape at phone and desktop sizes; the screen
  rectangle scales with the TV.
- e2e (desktop): power off blanks the picture and on restores it; a click on
  the channel dial's right half starts the next game; the wheel over volume
  changes it.
- e2e (phone): the remote appears after a tap, and a remote key reaches the
  Timendus keypad test.

**Done when:** README's M6 row holds, the TV looks right on desktop and
iPhone, and every control works by click, drag, wheel and keyboard.
